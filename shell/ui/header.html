Prototype scene props and immersive XR environments with primitive volumes, mapped surfaces, and rapid device-ready exports.

XReate is a browser-native sketchbook for blocking out props, scenography, and spatial prototypes for videogames and immersive XR experiences. It is dedicated to Federico Torre, whose work in cinema, theatre, and scenography made space feel alive before it was built.

This web app was conceived, designed, and iterated with Agentic Coding: human direction and critical review guide an AI-assisted implementation process.

Translation note: parts of the interface were drafted with AI and may contain inaccuracies.

By Francesco Perchiazzi

Built with Three.js ↗

Space is the interface.

What the UV tools are for. The UV modes are simplified educational abstractions for learning how surfaces, textures, and spatial relationships behave.

Why limitations are intentional. Constraints make construction, scale, and iteration legible. XReate is not a replacement for professional 3D modelling software.

For advanced modelling, animation, rigging, materials, or production pipelines, use dedicated tools such as Blender.

Tip: if you export USDZ, open it in Safari on iPhone/iPad for Quick Look AR.

Privacy: 100% client-side generation. No images or generated files are uploaded to any server.

For Federico — who taught me how worlds are imagined before they are built.

Federico Torre was an Italian actor whose work crossed theatre, cinema, radio, and television. His formation in New York at the Theater Lab and his attention to bodies in space continue to inform this project’s love of scenography and lived worlds.

XReate is dedicated to Federico Torre.

“Cinema captured me when I was young and never let me go.

I learned this craft between the pavement and New York: by living, observing, and studying at the Theater Lab.

Because cinema, before anything else, is life.

And this life continues in those I met, in those who cared for me.”
